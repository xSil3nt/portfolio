01Introduction

How I work
as an engineer.

I'm in my final year of Computer Engineering. My projects often involve both hardware and software, such as reading field sensors, sending their data over satellite, and displaying telemetry in a browser.

The examples here come from my sensor nodes, our team's payload drone, my agricultural robot simulation, and my OrcaSlicer contribution. I usually learn by building something, testing it, and working out why it doesn't behave as expected.

I can be too quick to start coding before I've written down the requirements or a test plan. I need to do that earlier, and give cost estimates and standards the same attention as the implementation.

02SWOT analysis

Current strengths
and gaps.

S / Strengths

Connecting hardware and software

I can work on a sensor's firmware and the software that receives its readings. I'm also comfortable picking up unfamiliar tools as I need them.

W / Weaknesses

More breadth than depth

Several of my projects are still prototypes. I have less experience with production systems, formal compliance, and working out what a design will cost.

O / Opportunities

Taking existing projects further

My robotics projects give me room to learn more about real-time systems. Code reviews and feedback from experienced engineers can help me spot assumptions I've missed.

T / Threats

Limited hardware, data, and time

A simulation can look convincing without matching what happens in the field. Access to hardware and field data limits what I can check, especially near a deadline.

What I'll change

I want to spend more time on the projects I already have. For the next one, I'll write the requirements and test plan before coding, then ask for a review of the results and limitations. I'll also include a cost estimate or a check against the relevant standards.
